Rattan Door Step 1: Exact Double-Door Sizing & 1x3 Pocket-Hole Frame Build 4 Identical Doors: 12.75" W x 26.0" H

Why 12.75" Wide x 26.0" High is the exact mathematical size when putting two doors on each box:

  • The Opening Width Math: Each of your two base boxes has a 24.5" Wide opening inside the 1x2 face frame. With a 1/2" hinge overlay on the left stile (+0.5") and right stile (+0.5"), the pair of doors must span 24.5" + 1.0" = 25.5" Total Width. Divided by 2 doors = 12.75" Wide per door (giving a crisp 1/16" center reveal gap so they don't rub).
  • The 1x3 Cut Math: A 1x3 select pine or poplar board is actually 2.5" wide x 0.75" thick.
    • Cut 2 Vertical Stiles to 26.0" Long.
    • Cut 2 Horizontal Rails to 7.75" Long (2.5" + 7.75" + 2.5" = 12.75" W).
  • Pocket-Hole Assembly: Set your Kreg jig to 3/4". Drill 2 pocket holes on the back face at each end of the 7.75" rails. Glue the ends and clamp/screw the rails between the 26.0" stiles on a flat surface.
Rattan Door Step 2: How to Route the "Stepped Rabbet Groove" on the Back 3/8" Wide x 3/8" Deep Stepped Groove

Do not try to cut loose grooves on individual sticks before gluing—and do not route a spline channel on the front face. Instead, route a 3/8" W x 3/8" D Rear Stepped Groove (Rabbet) after the rectangular frame is glued and screwed together:

  • 1. Chuck a 3/8" Rabbeting Bit into Your Router: A rabbeting bit has a spinning pilot bearing at the tip that rides smoothly along the inside window of your door frame, guaranteeing a uniform 3/8" wide step with zero fence required.
  • 2. Set Bit Depth to 3/8" (Half of the 3/4" Board): Since your 1x3 board is 3/4" (0.75") thick, setting the cutting depth to 3/8" (0.375") leaves a strong 3/8" front lip and carves a recessed 3/8" shelf on the back.
  • 3. Route Around the Back Inside Window: Lay the assembled door back-side up on your workbench. Run the router clockwise around the inside perimeter.
  • 4. Square (or Leave) the Corners & Paint the Frame NOW: Before touching the rattan, prime and paint your 4 routed wood frames in Swedish Blue. That way you never get blue paint splatters on the natural golden rattan!
Rattan Door Step 3: Soaking, Stapling & Drilling the 35mm Hinge Cups Drum-Tight Shrink Fit
  • 1. Cut & Soak Rattan in Warm Water (30–45 Mins): Cut 4 pieces of rattan webbing to 8.5" Wide x 21.75" High with scissors. Submerge them in your bathtub or sink in warm water for 35 minutes. Water expands the natural cane fibers and makes them pliable so they won't crack when bent into the groove.
  • 2. Staple Opposite Sides Under Tension: Shake off excess water and lay the wet rattan into the painted back groove. Staple the Top edge first with 3/8" T50 staples every 1.5". Pull taut and staple the Bottom edge, then alternate Left and Right sides while keeping the grid lines parallel to the frame.
  • 3. Glue & Pin the 1/4" Backing Strips: Run a thin bead of wood glue over the staples inside the groove and press your 1/4" x 3/8" pine lattice strips into the groove with a few 5/8" brads. Trim any stray cane ends flush with a utility knife. Within 4 hours of drying, the rattan shrinks as tight as a snare drum!
  • 4. Drill the Two 35mm Hinge Cups: Clamp your Kreg Concealed Hinge Jig onto the back of the outer 2.5" stile at 3.5" from the top and 3.5" from the bottom (plenty of room away from the pocket screws). Drill the two 35mm cups and screw in your Blum hinges.
